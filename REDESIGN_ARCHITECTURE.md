# SciHub Redesign: Phenomena-Driven, Collaborative, Adaptive System

## Core Principles (Based on Research & NGSS)

1. **Phenomena-driven**: All learning anchored in observable, puzzling phenomena
2. **Collaborative by design**: Small group investigation → Individual sensemaking
3. **Diagnostic-responsive**: Daily system adapts to individual student performance
4. **Structured agency**: Scaffolding fades as competency is validated
5. **Talk-focused**: Science happens through argumentation and discourse
6. **Integrated dimensions**: SEPs, DCIs, CCCs woven together, not tagged separately

## System Flow: How a Unit Works

### Phase 1: Phenomena Encounter (Days 1-2)
**What happens:**
- Students observe compelling phenomenon (video, demo, local observation)
- Individual noticing (What do you observe? What patterns?)
- Small group noticing shares (structured protocol)
- Class generates questions (not teacher-provided driving question)
- Students vote/prioritize questions to investigate

**Notecard (Day 1):**
- Front: Sketch the phenomenon with labels
- Back: "What's one thing that surprised you? What's one question you have?"
- **Adaptive**: System flags students who struggle with observation vs. inference

**NGSS Integration:**
- SEP: Asking Questions (whole-class protocol)
- CCC: Patterns (what patterns did you notice?)
- DCI: Emerges from phenomenon (not pre-taught)

### Phase 2: Investigation Design (Days 3-4)
**What happens:**
- Teacher presents 2-3 investigation options (varying complexity)
- Small groups plan investigation (structured planning template)
- **Gap-based grouping**: Algorithm pairs students based on diagnostic data
  - Example: Strong questioner + strong data analyzer + needs both
- Groups pitch investigation plans (claim-evidence structure)
- Peer feedback protocol with sentence stems

**Notecard (Day 3):**
- Front: Your investigation question + your prediction (with reasoning)
- Back: What data will convince you? What might you observe?
- **Adaptive**: System adjusts prompts based on student's reasoning level (Bloom's)

**NGSS Integration:**
- SEP: Planning investigations, constructing explanations
- CCC: Cause and effect (if we do X, then Y because...)
- DCI: Students accessing prior knowledge to make predictions

### Phase 3: Collaborative Investigation (Days 5-8)
**What happens:**
- Small groups conduct investigations (3-4 students, assigned roles)
- **Structured talk protocol during data collection:**
  - Roles: Data collector, recorder, questioner, timekeeper (rotate daily)
  - Scripted prompts: "What did we observe? What does this mean? What should we try next?"
- Teacher circulates, takes diagnostic notes on individual students:
  - Who asks productive questions?
  - Who struggles with measurement/data recording?
  - Who makes connections to prior learning?
  - Who participates/withdraws from group work?
- Daily group consensus checkpoint: "What do we know so far?"

**Notecard (Days 5-8) - ADAPTIVE PER STUDENT:**

*Example 1: Student struggled with data interpretation in class*
- Front: Draw or paste your group's data table/graph
- Back: "What pattern do you see? What might explain this pattern?"

*Example 2: Student excelled but didn't connect to prior learning*
- Front: What did your group discover today?
- Back: "How does this connect to [previous unit/concept]? What's similar or different?"

*Example 3: Student dominated group discussion*
- Front: What idea did a GROUP MEMBER share that changed your thinking?
- Back: "How did listening to them help you understand better?"

*Example 4: Student withdrawn from group*
- Front: What's one question you wanted to ask today but didn't?
- Back: "What data would help answer it? Who in your group might help?"

**NGSS Integration:**
- SEP: Planning, investigating, analyzing data, constructing explanations
- CCC: Varies by investigation (scale, systems, cause-effect, etc.)
- DCI: Students building understanding through doing

### Phase 4: Sensemaking & Argumentation (Days 9-11)
**What happens:**
- Groups analyze data and construct explanations (claim-evidence-reasoning)
- **Jigsaw protocol based on knowledge gaps:**
  - System identifies: Who needs help with claims? Evidence? Reasoning? Connections to DCI?
  - Regroup students so each group has mix of strengths/needs
  - Expert shares their strength, learns from others' strengths
- **Highly structured argumentation session:**
  - Sentence stems provided:
    - "I claim that _____ because _____"
    - "My evidence is _____ which shows _____"
    - "I respectfully disagree because _____"
    - "Can you explain why you think _____?"
    - "Your evidence supports my idea because _____"
  - Talk moves explicitly taught and practiced:
    - Revoicing: "So you're saying..."
    - Pressing for reasoning: "Why do you think that?"
    - Prompting for evidence: "What makes you say that?"
- **Skill validation checkpoint:** Teacher assesses argumentation quality
  - Can student make a claim?
  - Can student cite appropriate evidence?
  - Can student provide scientific reasoning?
  - Can student critique others' reasoning respectfully?

**Notecard (Day 9-10) - ADAPTIVE:**

*If student ready for higher Bloom's:*
- Front: Draw a model that explains the phenomenon
- Back: "Use evidence from your data to explain WHY your model works"

*If student needs more structure (mid-Bloom's):*
- Front: Complete: "I claim that [phenomenon happens] because _____"
- Back: "My evidence is: 1) _____ 2) _____ 3) _____"

*If student needs foundational work (lower Bloom's):*
- Front: List 3 pieces of evidence from your investigation
- Back: "Which piece of evidence is strongest? Why?"

**NGSS Integration:**
- SEP: Arguing from evidence, constructing explanations
- CCC: Cause and effect, mechanism
- DCI: Students building conceptual understanding

### Phase 5: Peer Review & Revision (Days 12-13)
**What happens:**
- **Peer review ONLY if skills validated in Phase 4**
- Students swap explanations with partner (teacher assigns based on compatible skill levels)
- **Structured peer review protocol:**
  - Checklist provided: Does explanation have claim? Evidence? Reasoning? Clear connection to phenomenon?
  - Sentence stems: "One strength is _____. One suggestion is _____. One question I have is _____"
  - Students must justify feedback with criteria
- Students revise explanations based on feedback
- Self-assessment: "What improved? What's still challenging?"

**Notecard (Day 12-13):**
- Front: What feedback did you receive? What will you revise?
- Back: What feedback did you GIVE? Why was it helpful?
- **Adaptive**: System tracks metacognitive development

**NGSS Integration:**
- SEP: Engaging in argument, obtaining/communicating information
- CCC: Depends on explanation focus
- DCI: Deepening through revision

### Phase 6: Summative Performance (Days 14-15)
**What happens:**
- **Summative assessment = Performance task at high Bloom's (Create/Evaluate)**
- Options (student choice based on validated skills):

  *Option A: New Phenomenon Transfer*
  - Present similar phenomenon, students explain using their model
  - Individual work, assessed on explanation quality

  *Option B: Design Challenge*
  - Apply understanding to solve related problem
  - Can be individual or group with individual accountability

  *Option C: Teach Someone*
  - Create explanation for younger student (video, poster, model)
  - Assessed on clarity, accuracy, evidence use

- **Graduated agency applied here:**
  - Students who validated all skills: Choose any option, self-design format
  - Students who validated most skills: Choose from 3 options, teacher-provided format
  - Students who need more support: Teacher-assigned option with structured template

**Notecard (Day 14-15) - SUMMATIVE:**
- Front: Demonstrate your full understanding (open format)
- Back: How did your thinking change from Day 1 to now? What evidence convinced you?
- **Graded on Bloom's levels**: Create (highest), Evaluate, Analyze, Apply, Understand

**NGSS Integration:**
- All three dimensions integrated in authentic performance
- Assessment IS doing science, not recalling facts

## Technical System Requirements

### 1. Diagnostic Engine
**Continuously tracks per student:**
- **SEP competencies** (8 practices, each rated 1-4):
  - Asking questions (productive? testable? phenomenon-focused?)
  - Planning investigations (variables identified? logical procedure?)
  - Analyzing data (patterns recognized? quantitative reasoning?)
  - Constructing explanations (claim-evidence-reasoning structure?)
  - Arguing from evidence (respectful? evidence-based? logical?)
  - Using models (accurate? explains mechanism? predictive?)
  - Engaging in critique (constructive? criteria-based?)
  - Communicating (clear? audience-appropriate? evidence included?)

- **DCI understanding** (concept-specific, assessed through use):
  - Recalls key ideas (Bloom's: Remember)
  - Explains in own words (Bloom's: Understand)
  - Applies to new context (Bloom's: Apply)
  - Analyzes relationships (Bloom's: Analyze)
  - Evaluates models/claims (Bloom's: Evaluate)
  - Creates new explanations (Bloom's: Create)

- **CCC application** (7 concepts, rated on use in context):
  - Patterns, Cause-Effect, Scale, Systems, Energy-Matter, Structure-Function, Stability-Change
  - Can identify in phenomenon?
  - Can use as lens for investigation?
  - Can apply across science domains?

- **Collaboration skills:**
  - Participates verbally (too much/too little/balanced)
  - Listens to peers (dismissive/receptive/building on ideas)
  - Takes on group roles (avoids/completes/leads)
  - Handles disagreement (withdraws/argues/negotiates)

- **Metacognition:**
  - Identifies own confusion/gaps
  - Self-assesses progress accurately
  - Adjusts strategies based on feedback
  - Reflects on learning process

### 2. Adaptive Notecard Prompt Engine
**Daily workflow:**
1. Teacher inputs brief notes during class (or checks pre-made tags):
   - "Struggled with data interpretation"
   - "Great question asking today"
   - "Didn't connect to prior learning"
   - "Dominated group discussion"
   - "Withdrawn from group"

2. System generates individualized notecard prompts for each student:
   - **If struggling with skill**: Prompt scaffolds that specific skill at appropriate Bloom's level
   - **If excelling**: Prompt pushes to higher Bloom's or cross-cutting application
   - **If collaboration issue**: Prompt addresses social aspect
   - **If metacognitive gap**: Prompt builds reflection/self-assessment

3. Student sees their personalized prompt when they log in after class

4. Student completes notecard (front/back, can include photo upload, drawing, text)

5. System flags notecards for teacher review based on:
   - Red flag: Student shows misconception or significant struggle
   - Yellow flag: Student needs specific feedback
   - Green: Student demonstrating proficiency (quick review)

### 3. Gap-Based Grouping Algorithm
**Before each collaborative phase:**
1. System analyzes current diagnostic data for all students
2. Identifies each student's:
   - **Strengths** (SEPs/DCIs/CCCs rated 3-4)
   - **Growth areas** (SEPs/DCIs/CCCs rated 1-2)
   - **Collaboration preferences/needs**

3. Algorithm creates groups (3-4 students) that:
   - **Maximize gap-filling**: Each student's strength fills another's gap
   - **Avoid same-gap clusters**: Don't group all students who struggle with data analysis
   - **Balance collaboration styles**: Mix talkers with quieter students intentionally
   - **Rotate over time**: Students work with different peers across units

4. Teacher can override algorithm but sees rationale for each group

**Example grouping:**
- Student A: Strong at asking questions (SEP), weak at data analysis (SEP)
- Student B: Weak at asking questions (SEP), strong at data analysis (SEP)
- Student C: Strong at constructing explanations (SEP), weak at critique (SEP)
- Student D: Weak at explanations (SEP), strong at critique (SEP)

→ Algorithm pairs A+B and C+D, then combines into group of 4 so cross-learning happens

### 4. Structured Talk Protocol Library
**Pre-built, research-based protocols:**

**Protocol 1: Consensus Circle (Whole Class)**
- Used for: Generating questions, sensemaking discussions
- Structure:
  1. Individual think time (1 min)
  2. Turn and talk with partner (2 min)
  3. Volunteer shares, teacher revoices
  4. Others add on: "I'd like to add..." or "I disagree because..."
  5. Class builds consensus or identifies productive disagreements
- Sentence stems provided on screen/posters

**Protocol 2: Accountable Talk Moves (Small Group)**
- Used for: Group work discussions during investigation
- Teacher-taught moves:
  - "Can you say more about that?"
  - "Can you give an example?"
  - "So you're saying... [revoice]"
  - "I agree/disagree because..."
  - "That connects to what [name] said about..."
- Roles assigned: One student is "Talk Monitor" with checklist

**Protocol 3: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) Argumentation**
- Used for: Explaining phenomenon, defending conclusions
- Structure:
  1. State claim clearly
  2. Provide evidence (minimum 2 pieces)
  3. Explain reasoning (why does evidence support claim?)
  4. Peer asks clarifying question using stems
  5. Original speaker responds or revises
- Template provided, gradually faded

**Protocol 4: Peer Review Critique Protocol**
- Used for: Giving feedback on written work
- ONLY used after students validate critique skills
- Structure:
  1. Read peer's work silently (3 min)
  2. Identify 1 strength using criteria checklist
  3. Identify 1 area for improvement using criteria checklist
  4. Write suggestion using sentence stems: "Have you considered...?" "What if you added...?"
  5. Exchange verbally, both students record feedback
- Requires: Clear success criteria, sentence stems, monitoring

**Protocol 5: Jigsaw Knowledge Share**
- Used for: Gap-based learning, building on strengths
- Structure:
  1. Home groups (gap-based grouping)
  2. Expert groups (students with same strength meet)
  3. Experts deepen their understanding of their strength area
  4. Return to home groups, each expert teaches their strength
  5. Home group produces collaborative product using all strengths
- Teacher provides expert group tasks, home group synthesis task

### 5. Skill Validation Modules
**Before students can engage in peer review or complex collaboration:**

**Module 1: Asking Productive Questions (Week 1 of school year)**
- Mini-lesson: Question types (testable vs. non-testable, open vs. closed)
- Practice: Students generate questions about phenomenon, teacher sorts publicly
- Practice: Students critique question quality using checklist
- Validation: Student generates 2 testable questions independently → Skill unlocked

**Module 2: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (Week 2-3)**
- Mini-lesson: CER structure with examples (strong vs. weak)
- Practice: Students identify claims/evidence/reasoning in sample paragraphs
- Practice: Students write CER for simple phenomenon
- Validation: Student writes CER that meets criteria checklist → Skill unlocked

**Module 3: Respectful Critique (Week 3-4)**
- Mini-lesson: Critique vs. criticism, using criteria not opinion
- Practice: Students critique sample work using sentence stems
- Practice: Students receive critique and respond productively
- Validation: Student gives criteria-based feedback + receives feedback gracefully → Skill unlocked

**Module 4: Data Analysis (Unit-specific)**
- Mini-lesson: Types of data displays, pattern recognition
- Practice: Students analyze sample data sets, identify patterns
- Practice: Students create appropriate data displays
- Validation: Student analyzes novel data set and draws valid conclusion → Skill unlocked

**Progress tracked in student profiles:**
- Skill: Asking Questions ✓ Validated (Date)
- Skill: CER ✓ Validated (Date)
- Skill: Critique ✓ Validated (Date)
- Skill: Data Analysis (Unit 1) ✓ Validated (Date)

**Peer work unlocks:**
- Peer review ONLY available if Critique validated
- Small group investigations require Questions + Data Analysis validated
- Jigsaw protocols require CER validated

### 6. Graduated Agency Unlock System
**How agency increases with demonstrated competency:**

**Level 1: Structured Novice** (Beginning of year, or new skill)
- Teacher assigns: Groups, investigation procedure, notecard prompts, due dates
- Student chooses: Topic from 2-3 options, minor customization within structure
- Format: Highly scaffolded templates, sentence stems, checklists

**Level 2: Guided Practitioner** (After 1-2 units, or skill validated)
- Teacher assigns: General investigation approach, group composition
- Student chooses: Specific research question, data collection methods, notecard approach, pace within window
- Format: Templates available but optional, success criteria clear

**Level 3: Supported Expert** (After 3-4 units, or multiple skills validated at high level)
- Teacher assigns: Phenomenon and learning goals (NGSS alignment)
- Student chooses: Investigation design, group members, notecard format, timeline, assessment format
- Format: Self-designed within parameters, student proposes format to teacher

**Level 4: Independent Scholar** (Advanced students or end of year)
- Teacher assigns: NGSS performance expectation(s) to demonstrate
- Student chooses: Phenomenon to investigate, all investigation aspects, assessment format, collaborators, timeline
- Format: Student-designed project proposal, must justify alignment to NGSS

**System tracks per student:**
- Current agency level per skill domain (SEPs can be different levels)
- Unlock criteria clear and visible to student
- Students can SEE what they need to demonstrate to unlock more agency
- Teachers can override system (grant more/less agency based on context)

### 7. Bloom's-Aligned Assessment Architecture

**Formative Assessment (Daily Notecards):**
- **Focus**: Remember, Understand, Apply (Lower-Mid Bloom's)
- **Purpose**: Check for understanding, identify misconceptions, build coherent ideas over time
- **Feedback**: Individualized, specific, actionable
- **Grading**: Not graded, or completion-based, or low-stakes (formative)

**Notecard Bloom's progression within a unit:**
- Days 1-3: Understand (observe, describe, explain in own words)
- Days 4-7: Apply (use idea in new context, make predictions)
- Days 8-11: Analyze (identify patterns, compare, contrast, categorize)
- Days 12-15: Evaluate/Create (judge quality, design solutions, build models)

**Summative Assessment (End of Unit Performance):**
- **Focus**: Analyze, Evaluate, Create (High Bloom's)
- **Purpose**: Demonstrate integrated three-dimensional learning
- **Format**: Performance task or project with clear rubric
- **Grading**: Standards-based rubric aligned to NGSS Performance Expectations

**Summative task design principles:**
- Must integrate all 3 dimensions (SEP + DCI + CCC)
- Must require application to novel context (transfer)
- Must allow multiple pathways to demonstrate competency
- Must assess DOING science, not recalling facts

**Example summative task (End of Ecosystems Unit):**
"A local park is experiencing algae blooms in the pond. Design an investigation to determine the cause, collect data, explain the phenomenon using your understanding of ecosystems and matter cycling, and propose a solution. Create a presentation for the park board that uses evidence to support your explanation and solution."

- **SEP**: Planning investigation, analyzing data, constructing explanation, communicating
- **DCI**: Matter cycles, ecosystems, populations, energy transfer
- **CCC**: Cause and effect, systems, scale
- **Bloom's**: Create (design investigation + solution), Evaluate (judge data quality, solution effectiveness), Analyze (patterns in data, system interactions)

### 8. Phenomena Architecture

**Two-track approach:**

**Track 1: Curated Phenomena Library**
- Pre-vetted phenomena aligned to NGSS Performance Expectations
- Includes:
  - Video/image of phenomenon
  - NGSS alignment tags (which PE, DCI, CCC, SEP best fit)
  - Suggested investigation paths (2-3 options at varying complexity)
  - Common student questions and misconceptions
  - Connection to prior learning
  - Differentiation notes
  - Real-world context and relevance
- **Purpose**: Saves teacher planning time, ensures NGSS alignment, provides high-quality anchors

**Track 2: Local Phenomena Tools**
- Teacher can add custom phenomena (upload video/image, add description)
- System prompts for:
  - Observable features (what will students notice?)
  - Puzzling aspect (what makes this phenomenon interesting/surprising?)
  - NGSS alignment (teacher tags relevant PE, DCI, CCC, SEP)
  - Student accessibility (can students observe this directly? Repeatedly?)
- System suggests:
  - Similar phenomena from library
  - Related investigations
  - Connected concepts from previous units
- **Purpose**: Allows responsiveness to local context, student interests, current events

**Phenomena quality criteria:**
- **Observable**: Students can see/hear/measure it
- **Puzzling**: Creates productive confusion or surprise
- **Relevant**: Connects to students' lives or important issues
- **Explainable**: Can be explained using grade-appropriate science ideas
- **Investigable**: Students can collect data to explain it
- **Generative**: Leads to more questions and deeper investigation

**Examples by NGSS domain:**

*Life Science:*
- Why do some plants grow in cracks in sidewalks but others don't?
- Why do our classroom fish swim together in groups?
- Why does bread dough rise?

*Physical Science:*
- Why does a basketball bounce higher on some surfaces?
- Why do some materials stick together and others don't?
- Why does steam from a pot of water disappear?

*Earth Science:*
- Why is the sky different colors at different times of day?
- Why are there different types of rocks in our schoolyard?
- Why does it rain more in some months than others?

## How This Addresses Your Concerns

### ✅ NGSS Alignment
- **Phenomena-first**: Every unit anchored in observable phenomenon
- **Integrated dimensions**: SEPs, DCIs, CCCs used together in authentic practice
- **Three-dimensional performance**: Summative tasks require integrated demonstration
- **Coherent storylines**: Daily notecards build connected ideas over time, not isolated facts

### ✅ Collaborative Learning Research
- **Talk is central**: Structured protocols ensure productive discourse
- **Peer learning**: Gap-based grouping maximizes peer-to-peer teaching
- **Argumentation**: CER protocol builds scientific argumentation skills
- **Social construction**: Sensemaking happens in groups, notecards reflect on collaborative learning

### ✅ Student Agency
- **Structured progression**: Agency increases as skills validated
- **Student-visible criteria**: Students know what unlocks more choice
- **Multiple pathways**: Students choose formats, questions, pace (within structure)
- **Authentic choice**: Not just picking from menus, but designing investigations (when ready)
- **Responsive**: Daily notecard adapts to individual needs, not one-size-fits-all

### ✅ Individual Daily Artifact
- **Personalized prompts**: Each student gets adaptive prompt based on their learning that day
- **Formative purpose**: Notecards build understanding, not just assess it
- **Reflection on collaboration**: Notecards help students process group work individually
- **Teacher insight**: Teacher sees each student's thinking, not just group products
- **Flexible format**: Students can sketch, write, photo, video depending on prompt
