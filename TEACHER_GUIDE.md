# 👩‍🏫 SciHub Teacher Implementation Guide

## Getting Started in Your Classroom

### Week 1: Setup & Introduction

**Day 1: Introduce the Notecard System**
- Show students example notecards (bring 2-3 high-quality examples)
- Explain: "Every day, you'll create ONE notecard that shows your scientific thinking"
- Emphasize: Front (visual) + Back (written explanation)
- Demo: Create a notecard together as a class

**Day 2: Introduce NGSS 3D Learning**
- Teach the three dimensions (use student-friendly language):
  - DCI: "What scientists know about the world"
  - CCC: "Big ideas that connect everything"
  - SEP: "What scientists actually DO"
- Have students identify all 3 in a simple example
- Create practice notecard with NGSS alignment

**Day 3: Launch First Project**
- Present 2-3 project options
- Students explore projects on Chromebooks
- Students make initial selection
- Start project work, create first "real" notecard

**Day 4-5: Build Routine**
- Continue project work
- Daily notecard creation becomes routine
- Collect cards at end of each class
- Provide quick verbal feedback

### Week 2: Deepen Engagement

**Focus on Feedback**
- Write feedback on 5-7 cards per day (not all at once!)
- Use feedback sentence stems:
  - "I notice you..."
  - "Your evidence shows..."
  - "To deepen this, try..."
  - "This connects to..."

**Introduce Digital Component**
- Students log into SciHub
- Practice photographing physical cards
- Enter card content digitally
- Explore their dashboard

**Start Peer Review**
- Pairs share notecards
- Use "2 Stars and a Wish" protocol
- Builds community and raises quality

## Daily Routine (Once Established)

### For You (Teacher)

**Morning (5 min)**
- Review which students need check-ins
- Prepare today's prompt (if customizing from project)

**During Class (ongoing)**
- Students work on projects
- Circulate and observe
- Mental notes for feedback

**Last 10 Minutes of Class**
- "Notecard time!" announcement
- Students create cards (front + back)
- Remind: NGSS alignment tags
- Collect physical cards
- Students submit digitally

**After School (10-15 min)**
- Review 5-7 cards in depth
- Skim remaining for red flags
- Write meaningful feedback
- Update dashboard

### For Students

1. See today's prompt (morning or start of class)
2. Work on project, make observations, collect data
3. Last 10 minutes: create notecard
4. Submit physical card to teacher
5. Enter card digitally in SciHub
6. Check feedback from yesterday

## Grading & Assessment

### Daily Notecard Rubric (Simple 4-Point)

**4 - Exceeds**
- Both sides complete and detailed
- Clear NGSS 3D integration
- Evidence of deep thinking
- Neat and organized

**3 - Proficient**
- Both sides complete
- NGSS alignment present
- Demonstrates understanding
- Readable

**2 - Developing**
- One side incomplete OR minimal effort
- Weak NGSS connection
- Surface-level thinking

**1 - Incomplete**
- Missing or unreadable
- No NGSS alignment
- Does not demonstrate learning

### Weekly Grade Calculation

**Option 1: Points-Based**
- 4 points x 5 days = 20 points per week
- 1 extra credit point for perfect week (all 4s)

**Option 2: Completion + Quality**
- 70% Completion (submitted all 5 cards)
- 30% Quality (average of rubric scores)

**Option 3: Standards-Based**
- Track mastery of NGSS standards over time
- Notecards provide evidence
- Report on standard progress, not individual cards

### Project Grade Components

- **Daily Notecards**: 40%
- **Final Product/Presentation**: 30%
- **NGSS 3D Demonstration**: 20%
- **Self-Assessment & Reflection**: 10%

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### "I don't have time to review 30 notecards every day!"

**Solutions:**
- You don't have to write feedback on every card daily
- Rotate: In-depth feedback on 5-7 different students per day
- Quick check for completion on all cards (1 min)
- Use student self-assessment and peer review
- Verbal feedback counts too!
- Dashboard helps identify who needs attention

### "Students rush and create low-quality cards"

**Solutions:**
- Model expectations repeatedly
- Share exemplar cards (with permission)
- Make quality part of grade
- Give feedback that raises standards
- Celebrate improvement publicly
- Use peer review to show range

### "Some students finish projects faster than others"

**Solutions:**
- Projects are self-paced by design
- Fast finishers: "Go deeper" extension prompts
- Fast finishers: Help peers or start choice project
- Use dashboard to assign appropriate next project
- Personalized pace is a feature, not a bug!

### "Students choose the same project everyone else did"

**Solutions:**
- Limit class to 2-3 projects running simultaneously
- Create "balanced choice" board showing project enrollment
- Highlight unique aspects of less-popular projects
- Offer small incentives for trying something different
- Remember: some sameness is okay for peer learning

### "Physical notecards get lost or damaged"

**Solutions:**
- Digital entry serves as backup
- Students keep cards in binder or box
- Photograph cards before submitting
- Return cards weekly for review sessions
- Make card care part of responsibility grade

## Maximizing Student Engagement

### Gamification Ideas

**Streaks**
- Celebrate 5-day, 10-day, 20-day streaks
- "Scientist Streak" badges
- Class streak leaderboard (optional)

**Mastery Tracking**
- Visual progress bars for NGSS standards
- "Standard Master" certificates
- Mastery wall display

**Card Quality**
- "Card of the Week" spotlight
- Student voting on best cards
- Gallery walks of excellent work

### Building Card Culture

**Weekly Card Gallery**
- Post 3-5 excellent cards on wall
- Students do "gallery walk" on Fridays
- Discuss what makes them strong

**Card Exchange**
- Students swap cards to study from
- Teaching each other strengthens both

**Parent Connection**
- Send home notecard stack monthly
- Parents initial after review
- Conversation starter for families

**Portfolio Building**
- Students select "best" cards quarterly
- Reflect on growth over time
- Present portfolio at conferences

## Differentiation Strategies

### For Struggling Students

- **Structured Templates**: Use highly scaffolded notecard templates
- **Sentence Starters**: Provide multiple options
- **Visual Supports**: Word banks, image libraries
- **Extended Time**: Allow card creation for homework
- **Partnership**: Pair with strong peer
- **Frequent Check-ins**: Daily brief conferences

### For Advanced Students

- **Open-Ended Prompts**: "Teach me something unexpected"
- **Cross-Project Connections**: Connect to other content
- **Original Questions**: Design own investigations
- **Mentorship**: Help create templates for class
- **Extension Projects**: Additional complexity layers
- **Research Integration**: Add outside sources

### For English Learners

- **Multilingual Cards**: Allow home language use
- **Visual Emphasis**: Heavy use of diagrams
- **Vocabulary Support**: Science word walls
- **Translation Tools**: Chromebook supports
- **Partner Work**: Strategic pairing
- **Scaffolds**: Graphic organizers

## Advanced Features

### Using the Spaced Repetition System

**Week 1**: Students create cards
**Week 2**: First review session (Friday or Monday)
- Students use SciHub review mode
- Rate recall: Easy/Medium/Hard
- System schedules next review

**Ongoing**: Students review 5-10 cards per week
- Can be homework
- Can be warm-up activity
- Builds long-term retention

### Data-Driven Instruction

**Use Dashboard to:**
- Identify students struggling with specific standards
- See which CCCs students use most/least
- Notice which SEPs need more practice
- Track engagement trends
- Plan interventions

**Monthly Data Meeting (with yourself):**
1. Pull class data from dashboard
2. Identify patterns
3. Adjust instruction
4. Set goals for next month

## Year-Long Pacing

### Suggested Timeline

**Trimester 1 (Sep-Nov)**
- Projects: Cell City, Water Quality Detective
- Focus: Establishing routines, quality expectations
- NGSS: MS-LS1-1, MS-ESS3-1, MS-ESS2-1

**Trimester 2 (Dec-Feb)**
- Projects: Chemistry Kitchen, Force & Motion in Sports
- Focus: Deepening rigor, peer collaboration
- NGSS: MS-PS1-2, MS-PS2-1, MS-ETS1-1

**Trimester 3 (Mar-May)**
- Projects: Student choice from remaining
- Focus: Independence, synthesis, mastery
- NGSS: Student-selected based on interests
